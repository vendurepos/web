## Worst defect per judge and criterion

### C1 First impression (mean 8.6)
- J1 (8): The hero lead ends in builder jargon a newcomer can't parse in five seconds: "Every sale lands in your Vendure store once, as a normal order at the till's price" — why 'once' matters and what 'the till's price' means only become clear two cards further down. _[home/desktop/01 and home/phone/01, hero paragraph]_
- J2 (8): The hero speaks to the freelancer, not the shop: 'Your own store needs the plugin on your Vendure server first' is the first instruction. Nothing above the fold says what the counter needs (a computer with Chrome, a card terminal you already have, a printer) or whether app.vendurepos.com costs anything. _[home.desktop.01 / home.phone.01, hero and CTA line]_
- J3 (10): none found _[text/home.txt; shots/home.desktop.01.jpg; shots/home.phone.01.jpg]_
- J4 (9): On phone the hero till screenshot is a tall crop showing only four product cards with the category chips cut off at 'Gif', so the hero plus image runs to about 1.3 screens before any content below it appears. _[home.phone.01-02]_
- J5 (8): The hero says what it is ('Point of sale for Vendure') but nowhere on home says who it is NOT for or how it sits against the obvious alternatives (Shopify POS, Square, Lightspeed, WCPOS/MedusaPOS siblings). The only maturity signal is the small 'Pre-release · plugin 0.3.0' pill; the footer line 'For Medusa, see MedusaPOS' is the only positioning. _[home.desktop.01 (hero), home footer]_

### C2 Credibility (mean 8.4)
- J1 (8): The comparison table says the demo sends "anonymous usage events to ph.wcpos.com" — a different brand's domain with no word on who wcpos is or how it relates to VendurePOS, which makes a first-time visitor wonder who is behind the site. _[home/desktop/02, 'Live demo or your own store' table, last row (also home/phone/04)]_
- J2 (8): Device support is vague for a counter decision. The page says only 'Its browser tests run in desktop Chrome; other browsers are not tested yet', so an owner can't tell whether the iPad or Android tablet at the counter will work tomorrow. _[home 'Before you start'; limitations.desktop.01]_
- J3 (10): none found _[text/home.txt; text/limitations.txt; text/connect-a-till.txt]_
- J4 (9): Of the ten 'What it does today' cards only the first two carry evidence links ('Read the acceptance test', 'Read the 200-send test'); the other eight are bare claims in identical cards, so the grid reads half-proven. _[home.desktop.01-02, 'What it does today' grid]_
- J5 (7): 'MIT licensed' plus 'To build and host it yourself instead of using app.vendurepos.com, you need your own RxDB Premium licence token (RXDB_PREMIUM)': the site never says RxDB Premium is a paid commercial licence or what it costs, and never says who operates app.vendurepos.com (or ph.wcpos.com, where the demo sends usage events), on what terms, or whether it will stay free. A sceptic reads this as an open-source label on a till that in practice depends on one unnamed party's hosted origin. _[home.desktop.02 'Before you start'; home 'Live demo or your own store' table (ph.wcpos.com)]_

### C3 Path to a till (mean 8.0)
- J1 (8): The path asks for nine steps, with permission lists to assemble by hand and a tax-strategy choice to make, and it has a documented gap: a device key's auto-created role "has the step 4 permissions except ReadOrder, so add ReadOrder to that role yourself". _[quick-start/desktop/02 step 4 and the 'Optional: a device key for each till' section (quick-start.txt)]_
- J2 (8): The quick start is complete and ends with a good 'Check it worked' list. But it runs to nine steps (migrations by tsx script, tax strategy table, CORS, permissions) with no time estimate and no hand-off line telling the freelancer what to give the shop staff once it works. _[quick-start.desktop.01-04, steps 3-6]_
- J3 (8): Step 6 prescribes different store-wide tax strategies for inclusive and exclusive channel pricing, but tells stores mixing those modes to 'account for the store-wide strategy' without specifying a supported configuration or verification procedure. _[shots/quick-start.desktop.03.jpg; text/endpoint_llms_full_txt.txt, lines 809–838]_
- J4 (8): The quick start never shows what success looks like: no screenshot of the till sign-in screen or of the first sale arriving as a Vendure order, and step 4's paragraph is a wall of eight inline code chips (TallyPosSell, ReadCatalog, ...). _[quick-start.desktop steps 4-8; quick-start.phone.04]_
- J5 (8): The path makes you patch the plugin's own role ('On plugin 0.3.0 that role has no ReadOrder: add it yourself'), and step 5 carries a leftover collapsible 'If your store already allows vendurepos.vercel.app' that refers to an origin the reader has never been told about. _[quick-start step 4 and step 5 (CORS); connect-a-till text]_

## Overall worst defect per judge

- **J1 First-time visitor** (C1): In the first five seconds the hero sentence explains the product in builder's terms ("lands in your Vendure store once, as a normal order at the till's price"). A visitor who only half knows Vendure gets the mechanism, not the benefit, and has to read the cards to learn why they should care. _[home/desktop/01 and home/phone/01, hero paragraph under 'Point of sale for Vendure']_ Fix: Make the lead benefit-first, for example: "Sell in person from any browser, even when the internet drops; every sale shows up in your Vendure store as a normal order, never twice." Leave the 'till's price' detail to the 'Paid, fulfilled, at the till's price' card.
- **J2 Working shop owner** (C2): A shop owner cannot tell whether the device at their counter will work. The only statement is 'Its browser tests run in desktop Chrome; other browsers are not tested yet', with no answer for a counter iPad, an Android tablet or a Chromebook. That is the first question for 'can staff sell with this tomorrow'. _[home 'Before you start'; limitations.desktop.01]_ Fix: Add one plain line on home and at the top of Limitations naming what is known to work at a counter (for example: desktop Chrome on Windows/Mac and Chromebook tested; Android Chrome and iPad Safari not tested yet; use a desktop or laptop with Chrome for now).
- **J3 Developer evaluating it** (C3): A developer with both tax-inclusive and tax-exclusive channels must guess how to satisfy the conflicting store-wide tax strategy recommendations. _[shots/quick-start.desktop.03.jpg; text/endpoint_llms_full_txt.txt, 'Why these pairings']_ Fix: Document the supported strategy for mixed pricing modes and how to verify totals in each mode. If that configuration is unsupported, state the limitation explicitly beside the table.
- **J4 Designer** (C5): The cashier-sheet and closure screenshots on At the till float small inside oversized empty grey frames (about 40% side padding), so the two key register images read as thumbnails while the payment shot on the same page fills its frame. _[at-the-till.desktop.03, at-the-till.desktop.04]_ Fix: Crop each image to the sheet itself (or render the frame at the sheet's natural width, about 400 px, centred) so the sheet fills its frame like the payment screenshot does; keep the same frame radius and border for all three.
- **J5 Competitor-aware sceptic** (C2): 'To build and host it yourself instead of using app.vendurepos.com, you need your own RxDB Premium licence token (RXDB_PREMIUM)' sits under an 'MIT licensed' footer with no word that RxDB Premium is paid, what it costs, who runs app.vendurepos.com and ph.wcpos.com, or on what terms. Add to that a live demo showing refund-at-till that no release has, with the last release on 2026-10-06 and no date for the next. A consultant would warn a client about vendor lock-in they cannot size. _[home.desktop.02 'Before you start'; changelog.desktop.01]_ Fix: Add one 'Who runs this and what it costs' line on home and in the overview: name the operator of app.vendurepos.com and ph.wcpos.com and their terms (free, uptime, how long), state that RxDB Premium is a paid licence with its price or a link, and add an 'Unreleased / next' entry in the changelog naming refund-at-till and a target date.
