## Worst defect per judge and criterion

### C1 First impression (mean 7.6)
- J1 (7): The home page never shows the product: there is no screenshot or picture of the till anywhere, only a headline, four equally weighted buttons (Live demo / Quick start / Try with your store / GitHub) and a wall of ten text cards using terms like 'idempotency ledger', 'Keyboard-wedge' and 'counted float'. A visitor who half knows Vendure cannot picture what they would be getting. _[home/desktop/01 and home/phone/01-02]_
- J2 (7): There is no picture of the till anywhere on the home page, so an owner can't see what staff will use without leaving the site. The hero also gives four equal-weight buttons ('Live demo', 'Quick start', 'Try with your store', 'GitHub'), and 'Try with your store' sounds like the owner's next step when it actually needs the plugin installed first. _[home.desktop.01 / home.phone.01 hero]_
- J3 (9): none found _[shots/home.desktop.01.jpg; shots/home.phone.01.jpg]_
- J4 (7): The home hero is all text: no screenshot of the till anywhere on the page. Its CTA row puts four buttons at nearly equal weight, three of them identical outline buttons ('Quick start', 'Try with your store', 'GitHub'), and 'Try with your store' repeats the nav link right above it, so what to click after 'Live demo' is unclear. _[home.desktop.01 hero; home.phone.01 (the button row wraps 2+2 with ragged widths)]_
- J5 (8): The home page never shows the till itself: the hero is a headline, a paragraph and four equal-weight buttons ('Live demo', 'Quick start', 'Try with your store', 'GitHub'), two of which ('Quick start' and 'Try with your store') look like the same next step, and the first visual below them is ten text-only grey cards. Next to Shopify POS or Square, a buyer cannot see in five seconds what the cashier screen looks like. _[home.desktop.01 hero and 'What it does today'; home.phone.01]_

### C2 Credibility (mean 7.6)
- J1 (8): The strongest proof claims ('Our acceptance run makes 25 sales, 20 of them offline...', 'The same sale sent 200 times makes one order') link to no test, run or source, so they read as assertions. And the plugin a visitor installs is called 'TallyPosPlugin', not VendurePOS, which is never explained on the home page. _[home/desktop/01 cards 'Keeps selling offline' and 'Every sale lands once'; quick-start/desktop/01 step 2]_
- J2 (7): 'Refunds: not yet' is a dealbreaker for a counter shop, but it appears only on the Limitations page. The home page's 'What it does today' lists ten capabilities, and the only route to the gaps is the small 'what it does not do yet' link at the very bottom. Limitations also never says what happens to sales still waiting to send if the till device is lost or its browser data is cleared. _[home.desktop.01-02 (What it does today, Before you start), limitations.desktop.01]_
- J3 (8): The precise claims about 25 sales and 200 resends have no accompanying link to the acceptance evidence. _[text/home.txt, What it does today]_
- J4 (8): The minimum plugin version contradicts itself. Troubleshooting says 'Run plugin 0.1.0 or later' for the 'missing or out of date' notice, while the quick start pins @vendurepos/plugin@0.3.0 and says the store must advertise 'order.create v4 and register v1'. _[troubleshooting.desktop.01 'Act on a header notice' 5th bullet vs quick-start.desktop.04 step 9]_
- J5 (7): The two strongest proof claims, 'Our acceptance run makes 25 sales, 20 of them offline, and checks that Vendure holds exactly 25 orders' and 'The same sale sent 200 times makes one order', link to nothing: no test file, no CI run, no repo path. Nothing on the site says who maintains the project or whether any shop runs it in production, and nothing says who should not use it (for example, a shop that needs refunds or integrated card terminals today). _[home.desktop.01, cards 'Keeps selling offline' and 'Every sale lands once'; tech.json home links (no test or CI link)]_

### C3 Path to a till (mean 7.4)
- J1 (7): Step 3 asks the reader to write and run their own TypeScript entry files for generateMigration/runMigrations 'with your project's existing TypeScript runner'. It names no file location and no command to run them, so the one step that changes the database is left to guessing. _[quick-start/desktop/01 step 3]_
- J2 (8): Step 8 tells you to sign in with 'your administrator email and password'. The safer per-till option (a device key with the vendurepos-pos-till role) is buried as an 'Optional' paragraph inside step 4 'Enable bearer authentication', so an owner's developer is steered toward handing cashiers admin logins. _[quick-start.desktop.02 (step 4 Optional) and quick-start.desktop.03 (step 8)]_
- J3 (8): Staff setup describes catalogue and settings access without giving the exact permission list; the concrete POS role permissions are buried in the changelog. _[text/plugin-setup.txt; text/connect-a-till.txt; text/changelog.txt, 0.2.0]_
- J4 (7): Step 4 'Enable bearer authentication' is required, but the optional device-key setup is poured into it: four paragraphs, a bullet list and a pnpm Vite config block, marked only by a bold 'Optional:' run-in. A first-time reader can't see where the required step ends, and connect-a-till already covers the same material. _[quick-start.desktop.02 (from 'Optional: a Vendure API key for each till' to 'npm projects need nothing extra.')]_
- J5 (7): Step 3 of the quick start makes the developer write two throwaway TypeScript entry files that call generateMigration and runMigrations, and run them 'with your project's existing TypeScript runner', which never names a command. It then sends the simpler bundled-migration route to 'the README' rather than the Plugin setup page that documents it. The Node requirement '^20.19 || >=22.12' is given only on Plugin setup, never in the quick start's prerequisites. _[quick-start.desktop.01, step 3; plugin-setup.desktop.01 'Check your store']_

## Overall worst defect per judge

- **J1 First-time visitor** (C1): There is no picture of the till anywhere on the home page (or in the docs). A first-time visitor from 'Vendure point of sale' reads a paragraph and ten text cards but never sees what the product looks like, so judging it means clicking away to the demo or leaving. _[home/desktop/01 and home/phone/01]_ Fix: Put one real screenshot of the till (cart and payment screen) directly under the hero buttons, linked to the live demo, with a smaller phone-sized crop on mobile.
- **J2 Working shop owner** (C2): The things a counter shop most needs to know it can't do yet (no refunds, the card terminal isn't connected, and a typed rather than verified manager approval) are kept off the home page. 'What it does today' shows ten capability cards with no matching 'not yet' list, so an owner only finds out about no refunds after their developer has installed it. _[home.desktop.01-02 / home.phone.01-04 (What it does today, Before you start)]_ Fix: Add a short 'Not yet' line or card next to 'What it does today' listing 'Refunds', 'Card terminal integration (you confirm card payments by hand)' and 'Coupon codes', each linking to /docs/limitations.
- **J3 Developer evaluating it** (C4): The recovery instructions leave the developer to determine when a partially retained sale should be resolved as applied or rejected. _[text/troubleshooting.txt, Resolve a sale the store kept part-way]_ Fix: Add a runnable tallyNeedsAdminCommands query and explain how to identify and inspect the associated order, payment and stock effects. State the conditions for each resolution and when the cashier should use Retry.
- **J4 Designer** (C6): On a phone, the one table in the quick start that a developer must copy from (step 6, choosing the order tax strategy) wraps the strategy class names character by character across three lines. The step that decides whether totals match is unreadable and uncopyable at 390 px. _[quick-start.phone.05 bottom to quick-start.phone.06 top]_ Fix: Put `white-space: nowrap` on code inside table cells and wrap the table in a horizontally scrolling container. Alternatively, render the two rows as stacked 'Tax-exclusive → new OrderLevelTaxCalculationStrategy()' lines below 640 px.
- **J5 Competitor-aware sceptic** (C2): The site's whole case against established tills rests on offline selling and exactly-once delivery, but the evidence ('Our acceptance run makes 25 sales, 20 of them offline...', 'The same sale sent 200 times makes one order') is unlinked prose. Nothing shows the till, who maintains it, or anyone running it, so I could not advise a client to trust it over Square or Shopify POS on this site's word. _[home.desktop.01, cards 'Keeps selling offline' and 'Every sale lands once']_ Fix: Link each of those two claims to the actual acceptance test and a recent CI run in vendurepos/app. Put one real screenshot of the till in the hero.
